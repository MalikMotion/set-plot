Interview lighting setup: a two-camera interview, step by step

By Malik Motion8 min readUpdated 11 November 2026

Quick answer

A good interview setup uses a large soft key on the side the subject looks toward, negative fill on the camera side to keep shape, a back light for separation, and practicals in the background for depth. Run an A camera on a medium shot and a B camera on a tighter angle so you can cut between them.

Interviews are the bread and butter of corporate, documentary and branded work. The setup below is the one many crews default to because it’s quick to build, flatters most faces and leaves the background interesting.

The cameras

The A camera sits close to the interviewer’s eye line on a 35–50 mm lens for a medium shot. The B camera sits at a wider angle from the eye line, on an 85 mm or longer, for a close-up. Keep both on the same side of the eye line so the subject looks the same way in both shots.

The light

  • Key: a large soft source (a dome or octa) on the side the subject is looking toward, so the far side of the face is lit. This is short-side key.
  • Negative fill: a black flag on the camera side adds shape and stops the room bouncing light back in.
  • Back light: a hard light with barn doors behind and above, opposite the key.
  • Background: warm practicals and a coloured tube give depth without lighting the whole wall.

Sound and comfort

Interviews run long. Keep lights out of the subject’s eye line, watch the heat from tungsten sources, and keep fans and ballasts away from the microphone.

Step by step

  1. Choose the backgroundPick a background with depth and place the subject at least 2 m from it.
  2. Set the eye lineSeat the interviewer just beside the A camera lens.
  3. Place the keyPut a large soft key on the side the subject looks toward.
  4. Add neg fill and back lightAdd negative fill on the camera side and a back light opposite the key.
  5. Light the backgroundDress the background with practicals and a coloured accent.
  6. Frame B cameraFrame the B camera tighter, from a wider angle, on the same side of the eye line.

Gear and common mistakes

What you need

  • A and B cameras with 50 mm and 85 mm lenses
  • Large soft key (light dome or octa)
  • Back light with barn doors
  • Black neg fill
  • Practical lamps, LED tube

Common mistakes

  • Key on the camera side, which flattens the face.
  • The subject too close to the background.
  • The two cameras on opposite sides of the eye line.

Frequently asked questions

Where should the key light be for an interview?

On the side the subject is looking toward (short-side key), about 45° off their eye line.

What lenses are best for interviews?

A 35–50 mm for the medium A shot and an 85 mm or longer for the B close-up are common choices on full frame and Super 35.

Do I need two cameras for an interview?

No, but a second angle lets you cut out pauses and mistakes without jump cuts.

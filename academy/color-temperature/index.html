Lighting basics

Color temperature on set: mixing daylight, tungsten and practicals

By Malik Motion7 min readUpdated 11 November 2026

Quick answer

Color temperature describes how warm or cool light is, measured in Kelvin (K). Tungsten and household lamps are warm (2700–3200K), daylight is cool (5600K and up). Set the camera’s white balance to the source you want to look neutral, and use CTO gels to warm a light or CTB gels to cool it so mixed sources look intentional.

Mixed light is one of the most common problems on location: a cool window, a warm lamp and an LED panel set somewhere in between. Once you know the numbers you can turn the mess into a look.

Common sources in Kelvin

  • Candle: about 1900K
  • Household lamp: 2700K
  • Tungsten film light: 3200K
  • Daylight at noon / HMI: 5600K
  • Overcast sky: 6500K
  • Shade and blue sky: 7000–10000K

White balance

The camera’s white balance decides which colour looks neutral. Set it to 3200K and tungsten looks white while daylight looks blue; set it to 5600K and daylight looks white while tungsten looks orange. Many cinematographers pick a value between the two, around 4300K, so the window goes slightly cool and the lamps slightly warm.

Gels

  • CTO (colour temperature orange) warms a light. Full CTO turns 5600K into about 3200K; half CTO into about 3800K.
  • CTB (colour temperature blue) cools a light. Full CTB turns 3200K into about 5600K, but it costs more than a stop of light.
  • Plus green / minus green corrects the green or magenta tint of fluorescent and some LED sources.

Bi-colour and RGB LEDs let you dial the Kelvin instead of gelling. Write the value into the plan so the next setup matches.

Step by step

  1. List every sourceNote each source and its Kelvin, including windows and practicals.
  2. Choose the lookDecide which source should look neutral.
  3. Set white balanceSet the camera white balance to that source or a value between your sources.
  4. Gel or dialGel or dial your lights to sit where you want them relative to that balance.
  5. Note the valuesWrite the values into the setup so you can repeat it.

Gear and common mistakes

What you need

  • Bi-colour or RGB LEDs
  • CTO and CTB gels (full, 1/2, 1/4)
  • Optional: colour meter

Common mistakes

  • Auto white balance changing during a shot.
  • Gelling with CTB on a weak light and losing too much output.
  • Ignoring the green tint from cheap LEDs and fluorescents.

Frequently asked questions

What is color temperature in film?

A measure of how warm or cool a light source is, in Kelvin. Low values are warm (orange), high values are cool (blue).

What Kelvin is daylight?

About 5600K for midday sun, higher in shade or on an overcast day.

What is CTO and CTB?

Gels that change colour temperature: CTO warms a light toward tungsten, CTB cools it toward daylight.

Rembrandt lighting: how to get the triangle of light

By Malik Motion6 min readUpdated 11 November 2026

Quick answer

Rembrandt lighting puts the key light about 45° to the side and 45° above the subject’s face, so the nose shadow joins the cheek shadow and leaves a small triangle of light under the eye on the shadow side. It needs only one light and gives a moody, painterly portrait.

The look is named after the Dutch painter, who lit many of his portraits from a high window to one side. It’s one of the fastest ways to make a face look three-dimensional and serious, and it’s used everywhere from drama to documentary interviews.

How to find the triangle

Start with the key at 45° to the side and raise it until it points down at the face at about 45°. Look at the shadow side of the face: you want a triangle of light on the cheek, no wider than the eye and no longer than the nose.

  • If the triangle is too big or the shadow side is mostly lit, move the light further round to the side.
  • If there’s no triangle, move the light back toward the camera or lower it slightly.
  • If the eye on the shadow side has no catchlight, the light is too high.

Fill or no fill

Classic Rembrandt uses no fill, or a negative fill (a black flag) to keep the shadow side deep. For a softer, more commercial version, add a white bounce on the shadow side at 2–3 stops under the key.

Hard or soft key

A small hard source makes the triangle crisp and graphic. A medium softbox with a grid keeps the shape but softens the edges, which suits most faces better on video.

Step by step

  1. Turn the faceHave the subject turn slightly away from the key.
  2. Place the key at 45°Put the key about 45° to the side of the subject’s nose.
  3. Raise the keyRaise the key and tilt it down about 45°.
  4. Find the triangleAdjust until the nose and cheek shadows meet and leave a triangle of light under the eye.
  5. Control the shadow sideAdd negative fill for drama, or a bounce for a softer look.

Gear and common mistakes

What you need

  • 1 key light, ideally a softbox with a grid
  • A black flag or neg fill
  • Optional: white bounce

Common mistakes

  • The light too high, so the eye sockets go black.
  • Too much fill, so the triangle disappears.
  • The subject turning toward the light during the take.

Frequently asked questions

What is Rembrandt lighting?

A portrait lighting pattern where the key light is high and to one side, leaving a small triangle of light on the shadow-side cheek.

How many lights do you need for Rembrandt lighting?

One. A key light and, optionally, a reflector or black flag on the other side.

What is the difference between Rembrandt and loop lighting?

In loop lighting the nose shadow is a small loop that doesn’t touch the cheek shadow. In Rembrandt lighting the two shadows join, leaving a closed triangle.

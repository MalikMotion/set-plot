Camera and planning

How to make a shot list and storyboard for a shoot

By Malik Motion7 min readUpdated 11 November 2026

Quick answer

A shot list is a numbered list of every shot you plan to film, with shot size, angle, lens, movement and a short description. A storyboard draws those shots as frames so everyone can see the sequence. Make the shot list first from the script, sketch the frames, then plan the camera positions on a top-down diagram so you can shoot in the most efficient order.

On the day, the shot list is the to-do list. If it’s clear, the first AD can schedule it, the gaffer knows what to light next and you can see at lunch whether you’ll make your day.

Shot sizes and what they’re for

  • Wide / establishing: where we are and who’s there.
  • Medium: body language and dialogue.
  • Close-up: emotion and reaction.
  • Extreme close-up: a detail the audience must notice.
  • Insert: hands, props, screens.

What goes on a shot list

Scene and shot number (1A, 1B…), shot size, angle, lens, movement, a one-line description, cast in the shot, and an estimated time. Group shots by camera position and lighting setup, not by script order: relighting is what eats the day.

From storyboard to animatic

Rough sketches are enough. In Set Plot you can draw frames with a pen or tablet, set each frame’s duration, add voice-over and music, and play the boards as an animatic to check timing before the shoot. The shot list and call sheet export as a PDF from the same project.

Step by step

  1. Break down the scriptBreak the script into scenes and beats.
  2. List the shotsList the shots each beat needs, with size, angle and lens.
  3. Sketch the framesSketch a storyboard frame for each shot.
  4. Plan the positionsPlan camera positions on a top-down diagram.
  5. Reorder and scheduleReorder the list by setup and lighting, then build the call sheet.

Gear and common mistakes

What you need

  • Script
  • Storyboard tool or paper
  • Top-down plan of the location

Common mistakes

  • Shooting in script order and relighting for every shot.
  • Shot lists without lens or size, so the camera team has to guess.
  • Too many shots for the time you have.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a shot list and a storyboard?

A shot list describes each shot in words; a storyboard shows them as drawings in sequence. Most productions use both.

Do I need to draw well to storyboard?

No. Stick figures with a clear frame size and direction of movement are enough.

What is an animatic?

A storyboard played back with timing and sound, so you can check pacing before filming.

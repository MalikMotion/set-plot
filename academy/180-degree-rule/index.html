Camera and planning

The 180-degree rule: keeping screen direction consistent

By Malik Motion5 min readUpdated 11 November 2026

Quick answer

The 180-degree rule says that in a scene between two people, the camera should stay on one side of an imaginary line drawn between them (the axis of action). Keep every camera on that side and each person stays on the same side of the frame from shot to shot. Cross the line and they appear to swap places, which confuses viewers.

It’s a rule about continuity, not about art. Plan your camera positions on a top-down plot and you’ll see the line straight away, before anyone has set up a tripod.

Covering a dialogue scene

A standard coverage plan uses a wide master plus an over-the-shoulder or close-up on each person, all from the same side of the line. Person A always looks screen right, person B always screen left, and the edit cuts smoothly between them.

How to cross the line safely

  • Move the camera across the line during a shot (a dolly or Steadicam move) so the viewer sees it happen.
  • Cut to a neutral shot straight down the line, then cross.
  • Let a character’s movement create a new line.

Breaking the rule on purpose can show confusion or a shift in power, but do it as a choice, not by accident.

Step by step

  1. Draw the lineDraw the line between the two characters on your plot.
  2. Pick a sideChoose a side for the whole scene.
  3. Place the camerasPlace the master, the over-the-shoulders and the close-ups all on that side.
  4. Plan any crossingIf you need to cross, plan a move or a neutral shot.

Gear and common mistakes

What you need

  • A top-down plan of the scene
  • Shot list with camera positions

Common mistakes

  • Shooting the reverse from the wrong side on a busy day.
  • Forgetting that eye lines must match when one character is off-screen.

Frequently asked questions

What happens if you break the 180-degree rule?

The characters appear to swap sides of the screen, which can make viewers think they’ve turned around or that the geography has changed.

Is the 180-degree rule always followed?

No. Directors break it on purpose to unsettle the audience, but it’s the default for clear continuity.

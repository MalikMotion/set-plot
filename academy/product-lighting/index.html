Product lighting: a tabletop setup for packshots

By Malik Motion6 min readUpdated 11 November 2026

Quick answer

A clean product setup uses a white sweep behind the product, a strip softbox as the key to draw a long highlight down one side, a bounce on the other side for fill and a gridded rim light from behind to outline the edges. Use a longer lens and a smaller aperture so the whole product stays sharp.

Products don’t move, so you can take your time and shape every highlight. The setup below is a good base for bottles, boxes, electronics and anything with clean edges.

Shaping highlights

Reflective products show the light source itself. A strip softbox makes a long, clean highlight that describes the shape of a bottle or a phone. Move it round the product until the highlight sits where you want it, then move the camera, not the light, for small changes.

Lens and focus

A 90–100 mm macro lens keeps perspective natural and lets you fill the frame from a distance. Stop down to f/5.6–f/11 for enough depth of field, or focus-stack for very small products.

Clean background

Light the sweep separately if you want pure white. Leave space between the product and the sweep so you can light them separately.

Step by step

  1. Set up the sweepPlace the product on a table in front of a white sweep, with some distance between them.
  2. Place the strip keyPut the strip key to one side and move it until the highlight is right.
  3. Add fillAdd a bounce on the other side.
  4. Add the rimAdd a gridded rim light from behind to outline the top and edges.
  5. Lock the cameraUse a long lens and a small aperture on a tripod.

Gear and common mistakes

What you need

  • Strip softbox (30×120)
  • White bounce
  • Small light with a grid
  • White sweep, table, macro lens, tripod

Common mistakes

  • Lights too small, so reflective surfaces show hot spots.
  • Product right against the sweep, so you can’t control the background.
  • Shooting wide open, so half the product is soft.

Frequently asked questions

What light is best for product photography?

Large soft sources such as strip boxes and softboxes, because they make clean, even highlights on reflective products.

How do I get a pure white background?

Light the background 1–1.5 stops brighter than the product, with separate lights, and keep the product away from it.
